What would my detector see from the Sun?

SNUfish computes solar neutrino interaction rates and day-night asymmetries for a chosen set of oscillation parameters, solar model, site, Earth model, interaction channel and detector response, and propagates the uncertainty of every input to the result: oscillation parameters and solar fluxes (with their correlations) are sampled; the choice between competing state-of-the-art models is shown as a range, never folded into a σ, and legacy models stay selectable but labelled and out of the ranges. The spectra, the sampled values and the error budget are plotted, and can be downloaded as data with plotipus commands.

What we have learned

  1. The tools agree; the inputs do not. Earth solvers agree to 10⁻⁸, the ν–e radiative corrections to 0.1 %.
  2. Most large spreads were outdated inputs. The factor 5–7 between ⁴⁰Ar tables, ADN up to 40 % high without a crust, +6.5 % on ⁸B from B16: legacy models and approximations, not uncertainties.
  3. Solar composition (B23 with MB22 or AAG21) is the open flux uncertainty: ⁸B 16 %, ⁷Be 8 %, CNO 30 %.
  4. ⁴⁰Ar: how much the Gamow–Teller strength is quenched, about 37 %: MARLEY 2 and the Suzuki–Honma shell model agree to 3 %, the Kostensalo shell model (gA = 1.00) is 27–35 % lower.
  5. Earth: the crust under the detector moves ADN by −2 to +4 %; the 3D Earth (up to ~8 %) is not yet computed, so no Earth uncertainty is counted.
  6. JUNO fixes Δm²₂₁; solar data alone sit 1.5σ lower: a physics question, not a choice of input.

How the numbers are made

  • Every input comes from a published model, measurement or tool, credited below and linked from the calculator's information buttons.
  • Oscillations in the Sun come from the SNUfish adiabatic reference solver; through the Earth from NuFast-Earth. The page interpolates tables of their results, which reproduce them to 2×10⁻⁴ for rates and 1 % for the day-night asymmetry.
  • The page's engine is a port of the SNUfish Python engine, checked against it to 10⁻¹¹ on the same inputs and random draws.
  • Rates are in observed energy after a generic detector response (threshold, Gaussian resolution, efficiency); there is no background model.

The benchmarks behind it

  • B1 standard solar models in one format; B2 oscillations in the Sun; B3 through the Earth; B4 cross sections; B5 interaction rates.
  • Each benchmark fixes its inputs, so tools that compute the same thing can be compared on the same plot.
  • The benchmark pages show what each step varies, how tools agree on it, and how much it moves the rates and the day-night asymmetry.
  • SNUfish is not a product of any collaboration. Please cite the original work of the results you use.

State of the art, legacy and approximations

Not every input SNUfish offers is current. state of the art inputs are the latest calculations and measurements; where several exist (the high- and low-metallicity solar models, the measured Gamow–Teller strengths of ⁴⁰Ar, two radiative-correction calculations) they compete, and their spread is the model range the calculator reports. legacy inputs have been superseded and are kept to reproduce published numbers; approximations simplify a current input. A difference between a legacy or approximate input and the state of the art is not an uncertainty, so the calculator labels both, keeps them out of the model ranges and says when a result uses one.
