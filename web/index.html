What would my detector see from the Sun?

SNUfish computes solar neutrino interaction rates and day-night asymmetries for a chosen set of oscillation parameters, solar model, site, Earth model, interaction channel and detector response, and propagates the uncertainty of every input to the result: oscillation parameters and solar fluxes (with their correlations) are sampled; the choice of cross-section model, Earth model and solar model is shown as a range, never folded into a σ. The spectra, the sampled values and the error budget are plotted, and can be downloaded as data with plotipus commands.

How the numbers are made

  • Every input comes from a published model, measurement or tool, credited below and linked from the calculator's information buttons.
  • Oscillations in the Sun come from the SNUfish adiabatic reference solver; through the Earth from NuFast-Earth. The page interpolates tables of their results, which reproduce them to 2×10⁻⁴ for rates and 1 % for the day-night asymmetry.
  • The page's engine is a port of the SNUfish Python engine, checked against it to 10⁻¹¹ on the same inputs and random draws.
  • Rates are in observed energy after a generic detector response (threshold, Gaussian resolution, efficiency); there is no background model.

The benchmarks behind it

  • B1 standard solar models in one format; B2 oscillations in the Sun; B3 through the Earth; B4 cross sections; B5 interaction rates.
  • Each benchmark fixes its inputs, so tools that compute the same thing can be compared on the same plot.
  • SNUfish is not a product of any collaboration. Please cite the original work of the results you use.
