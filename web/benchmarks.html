Where the uncertainty of a solar neutrino result comes from

A rate or a day-night asymmetry is built from five steps, each fixed by a SNUfish benchmark: the solar model (B1), the oscillations inside the Sun (B2), the crossing of the Earth (B3), the cross section (B4), and the folding into a rate (B5). Each step has its own spread: published uncertainties, a choice between models, or an approximation that some tools make. The table attributes every source of the calculator's error budget to the step it comes from, for five reference observables; each benchmark's page shows what varies and how tools agree on it.

Largest effect of each step, relative to the central value

Sampled: the 1σ spread from published uncertainties. Model range: the largest deviation from the nominal among the published alternatives (not a σ). Approximation: the shift a common simplification makes, a bias of a tool rather than an uncertainty. The oscillation parameters enter B2 and B3 and are shown on their own.

B1 · Standard solar models

Ten published standard solar models (B16, BS05, B23 with different surface compositions) in one format: the flux of every component with its uncertainty, the electron density ne(r) and where each component is produced. B1 sets the normalisation of every rate: the flux uncertainty (sampled, with the B23 correlations) and the spread between models (the composition problem) are the largest inputs to most rates. The density and production profiles enter through B2.

B2 · Oscillations inside the Sun

For the measured parameters the propagation out of the Sun is adiabatic: a neutrino leaves as a mass eigenstate with probability |Vei|² set by the electron density where it was produced. Each component is produced in its own region (⁸B at 0.05 R☉, ⁷Be at 0.07, pp at 0.12, hep at 0.14), so each has its own Pee(E). SNUfish averages over each component's production profile. Solvers that put every component at one point, or on ⁸B's profile, shift the result; the shift is largest for hep, produced furthest out (Lopes & Turck-Chièze, arXiv:1302.2791; Zaidel & Beacom, arXiv:2504.10583).

Tools on the same inputs

B3 · Crossing the Earth

At night the mass eigenstates cross the Earth and partly regenerate νe: the day-night asymmetry. B3 compares P(νi → νe) through PREM in three levels of detail (ten regions, four regions, Prob3++'s four shells), averaged over each site's night. The Earth model hardly moves a rate but is, with the oscillation parameters, the main uncertainty of the asymmetry.

Tools on the same inputs

B4 · Cross sections

Elastic scattering on electrons is known to well under 1 %: radiative corrections (Bahcall, Kamionkowski & Sirlin) and SNOwGLoBES's tables differ by a few per mille. νe CC on ⁴⁰Ar is not: the nuclear models disagree by tens of per cent, and within MARLEY the measured Gamow-Teller strengths of each ⁴⁰K level give a sampled uncertainty of several per cent. For argon detectors B4 is the largest uncertainty after the flux.

B5 · Interaction rates

B5 folds B1 fluxes, B2 oscillations and B4 cross sections into interactions per kiloton-year. It adds no input of its own, so no uncertainty in the table: what it tests is the folding. Tools that compute the whole chain (SNOwGLoBES) are compared with SNUfish's folding of exactly the same fluxes and cross sections.
