Where the uncertainty of a solar neutrino result comes from

A rate or a day-night asymmetry is built from five steps, the steps of the calculator, each fixed by the SNUfish benchmark of the same number and name: B1 Oscillation parameters, B2 Sun (the solar model and the oscillations inside the Sun), B3 Earth (its crossing at night), B4 Interaction (the cross sections) and B5 Detector (the folding into a rate and the detector response). Each step has its own spread: published uncertainties, a choice between models, or an approximation that some tools make. The table attributes every source of the calculator's error budget to the step it comes from, for five reference observables, counting state-of-the-art inputs only: published uncertainties, the spread between competing current models and, for B5, the statistics of 100 kt·yr and the published detector uncertainties. Each observable is seen through its detector's published response (Super-K IV, DUNE, Borexino Phase-II: the B5 benchmark's). Approximations and legacy models are shown on each benchmark's own page.

Largest effect of each step, relative to the central value

Sampled: the 1σ spread from published uncertainties. Model range: the largest deviation from the nominal among the competing state-of-the-art alternatives (not a σ); legacy models are plotted and labelled on each step's page but never counted here. B3: the two current crust models, under the detector and along each chord. B5: the statistical 1σ of 100 kt·yr (10 kt for 10 years) and the detector's published energy-scale, resolution and normalisation uncertainties (Super-K IV's for water; DUNE and Borexino publish none for solar neutrinos).

B1 Oscillation parameters · measurements and fits in one format

The solar parameters Δm²₂₁ and sin²θ₁₂ of each measurement and global fit, with their 1σ, in one format; the parameters a set does not determine (θ₁₃, θ₂₃, δ, Δm²₃₁) are NuFIT 6.0's. Like the solar models of B2 these are inputs, not computations: no tool is compared here. B2 and B3 run on them, and the calculator samples the chosen set's 1σ. Four sets are the state of the art: JUNO alone, NuFIT 6.1 with JUNO, and the solar data alone (all solar, and Super-K + SNO). Δm²₂₁ from the Sun sits about 1.5σ below JUNO's: a physics question (solar against reactor), not a choice of input, so the sets are never combined into a range.

Parameter sets

B2 Sun · solar models and the oscillations inside the Sun

Solar models. Ten published standard solar models (B16, BS05, B23 with different surface compositions) in one format: the flux of every component with its uncertainty, the electron density ne(r) and where each component is produced. They set the normalisation of every rate: the flux uncertainty (sampled, with the B23 correlations) and the spread between models (the composition problem) are the largest inputs to most rates. Three of the ten are the state of the art: B23 with the MB22 abundances (meteoritic and photospheric scales; high metallicity, favoured by helioseismology and the Borexino CNO rate) and B23 with AAG21 (low metallicity). Their spread is the composition problem as it stands; the other seven are legacy, labelled so, and kept to reproduce published numbers.

Oscillations inside the Sun. For the measured parameters the propagation out of the Sun is adiabatic: a neutrino leaves as a mass eigenstate with probability |Vei|² set by the electron density where it was produced. The solvers agree to 3 × 10⁻⁸, so the solar model, not the solver, is what moves a result. Each component is produced in its own region (⁸B at 0.05 R☉, ⁷Be at 0.07, pp at 0.12, hep at 0.14), so each has its own Pee(E). SNUfish averages over each component's production profile. Solvers that put every component at one point, or on ⁸B's profile, shift the result; the shift is largest for hep, produced furthest out (Lopes & Turck-Chièze, arXiv:1302.2791; Zaidel & Beacom, arXiv:2504.10583).

Tools on the same inputs

B3 Earth · crossing the Earth at night

At night the mass eigenstates cross the Earth and partly regenerate νe: the day-night asymmetry. B3 compares P(νi → νe) through PREM in three levels of detail (ten regions, four regions, Prob3++'s four shells), averaged over each site's night. On constant shells the four solvers agree to 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵ (PEANUTS's matter potential is rounded); on PREM's continuous regions nuSQuIDS integrates the polynomials and agrees with the reference to 10⁻⁶, while NuFast-Earth, which computes the calculator's night, takes each crossing's mean density, off by up to 2 % in the ⁸B ADN at 10–15 MeV against the reference (fourth-order Magnus, converged). The calculator's night therefore cuts those regions into 50 km shells (within 0.05 %); the bar "mean density per region crossing" is what NuFast-Earth's own treatment would shift. The state of the art is PREM with the CRUST1.0 crust, in two competing models: the column under the detector (radial) and the crust along each night chord (3D, near the detector and where the chord enters). Against full PREM they move ADN by −2.5 to +3.7 %; against each other by at most 0.2 %, B3's model range: the far side of a chord averages out. Not yet included: a second crust model (LITHO1.0, ~0.05 pp different at LNGS and SURF in Han et al.) and a 3D mantle. The crustless models are 20–40 % high; the other bars are approximations, signed shifts against the default.

B4 Interaction · cross sections: ν–e scattering, CC and NC

Elastic scattering on electrons is known to well under 1 %: radiative corrections (Bahcall, Kamionkowski & Sirlin) and SNOwGLoBES's tables differ by a per mille or less on the rates (up to 0.5 % point by point, νμ and ντ in opposite directions); tree level, an approximation, is 2–4 % high. νe CC on ⁴⁰Ar is not. MARLEY 2, with each of the three sets of measured Gamow–Teller strengths, and the shell model of Suzuki & Honma agree within 11 %; the shell model of Kostensalo, Suhonen & Zuber, which quenches the strength (gA = 1.00), is 27–35 % lower. That 37 % range is the largest model uncertainty for argon detectors. The older tables (KLMV, SNOwGLoBES's, MARLEY v1) are legacy: plotted for reference, not counted.

Tools on the same inputs

B5 Detector · interaction rates and the detector response

B5 has two parts. Part 1 folds the B2 fluxes and oscillations and the B4 cross sections into interactions per kiloton-year in true neutrino energy, the input of every detector. Part 2 passes them through each detector's published response (resolution, efficiency, threshold) into observed spectra and rates above threshold: Super-K IV for water, SNO's salt phase for heavy water, Borexino Phase-II for scintillator and DUNE's far detector for argon. Its entry in the overview is the statistics of 100 kt·yr and the published detector uncertainties. Tools that compute the whole chain (SNOwGLoBES) are compared with SNUfish's folding of exactly the same inputs; a tool may also contribute observed rates, with the spec's response or its own.

Discrepancies · the calculator's result templates

Every result template of the calculator sets each step to a paper's inputs. Each one is computed here twice, to keep apart two kinds of divergence:

  • Reproduction: SNUfish with the paper's own assumptions (its solar model, oscillation parameters, cross section, Earth model) against the number the paper publishes. What remains is what SNUfish cannot reproduce: rounding, an undocumented choice of the paper, or a difference of method; each template's note says which.
  • Update: SNUfish again, with every legacy input and approximation replaced by the state of the art (which inputs are which), against SNUfish with the paper's. That is what newer models and measurements change in the prediction: not an error of the paper, and not an uncertainty. A paper's own oscillation parameters are kept: they are the scenario its number answers (an asymmetry at a stated Δm²).

For measurements, the first table gives the pull: how far each prediction lies from the measured value, in its error. Day-night asymmetries are in the papers' convention, (D − N)/mean. See also docs/crosschecks.md.
