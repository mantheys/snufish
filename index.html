Solar Neutrino Rate and Uncertainty Tools

SNUfish computes solar neutrino interaction rates and day-night asymmetries for a chosen set of oscillation parameters, solar model, site, Earth model, interaction channel and detector response, and propagates the uncertainty of every input to the result: oscillation parameters and solar fluxes (with their correlations) are sampled; the choice between competing state-of-the-art models is shown as a range, never folded into a σ, and legacy models stay selectable but labelled and out of the ranges. The spectra, the sampled values and the error budget are plotted, and can be downloaded as data with plotipus commands.

What we have learned

B1

Reactors fix Δm²₂₁; the Sun prefers it 1.5σ lower. JUNO measures 7.50 × 10⁻⁵ eV² to 1.6 %, solar data alone give 6.1 × 10⁻⁵ eV² to about 15 %, and both agree on sin²θ₁₂. The gap is physics to resolve, not an input to choose.

B2

Solar composition is the largest open flux uncertainty. AAG21 predicts 16 % less ⁸B, 8 % less ⁷Be and 30 % less CNO than MB22m, each beyond MB22m's own 1σ. MB22p and MB22m agree to 1 %.

B3

The crust near the detector shifts ADN by −2.5 % to +3.7 %, with a size and sign set by the site. Taking it along each night chord instead of under the detector moves it by at most 0.2 %: that is the Earth range, with one crust model. Han et al.'s 3D Earth (another crust, LITHO1.0, on a 3D mantle) differs from it by 3–4 % of ADN at LNGS and SURF; neither is included yet.

B4

⁴⁰Ar CC is uncertain by 37 % because the Gamow–Teller quenching is unsettled. MARLEY 2 and the Suzuki–Honma shell model agree within 3 %. Kostensalo's shell model is 27–35 % lower with gA = 1.00 and agrees with the free gA. A stopped-pion measurement would settle it.

B5

SNOwGLoBES matches SNUfish to 0.4 % for ⁸B and hep, not below 2 MeV; the detector response matters more. On the same fluxes and cross sections its full chain is 16–21 % lower for ⁷Be, pep and ¹³N (GLoBES takes flux × σ once per 0.5 MeV bin). Through the published responses, Super-K IV keeps 52 % of the ⁸B ES rate above its threshold; DUNE's and SNO's resolutions take 4.4 % and 3.6 % off the CC rate.

B1B2B3B4

The tools agree; the inputs do not. Through the same Earth, NuFast-Earth, nuSQuIDS and PEANUTS agree with the reference to 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁵; out of the Sun, NuFast-Earth and PEANUTS to 3 × 10⁻⁸ and 10⁻⁴; the two ES radiative corrections to 0.1 %. That is orders of magnitude below the spread of competing inputs, and what is left is each tool's hard-coded constants. The largest tool effect, NuFast-Earth's mean density per crossing on PREM, moves the ⁸B ADN by 0.3 %; the calculator's night cuts PREM into 50 km shells, which removes it.

B2B3B4

Most large spreads were outdated inputs, not uncertainties. SNOwGLoBES's ⁴⁰Ar tables differ by a factor 5–7, Earth models without a crust overstate ADN by up to 40 %, and B16 gives 6.5 % more ⁸B. SNUfish labels these legacy or approximations and keeps them out of the ranges.

B1B2B3B4B5

One or two inputs limit each prediction. ES rates are limited by the solar model (16 % for ⁸B, 8 % for ⁷Be; the SNO NC ⁸B flux takes 16 % to 4 %), CC on ⁴⁰Ar by the nuclear model (36 % for ⁸B) and, for hep, equally by its flux (29 %), and ADN by statistics: 64 % at 100 kt·yr in water. The oscillation parameters add under 3 %; the Earth model, among current models, 0.1 % or less.

Every input's status and the literature behind it: About and docs/status-of-inputs.md. The figures are drawn from the benchmark data; the benchmark pages have the full comparisons.

How the numbers are made

  • Every input comes from a published model, measurement or tool, credited below and linked from the calculator's information buttons.
  • Oscillations in the Sun come from the SNUfish adiabatic reference solver; through the Earth from NuFast-Earth, on 50 km shells (chord by chord for the crust along each chord). The page interpolates tables of their results, which reproduce them to 2×10⁻⁴ for rates and 1 % for the day-night asymmetry.
  • The page's engine is a port of the SNUfish Python engine, checked against it to 10⁻⁹ on the same inputs and random draws.
  • Rates are in observed energy after a detector response (threshold, Gaussian resolution or a smearing matrix, efficiency): a published preset or your own. There is no background model.

The benchmarks behind it

  • The benchmarks are the calculator's steps after the result, in the same order and under the same names:
    • B1 Oscillation parameters: measurements and global fits in one format.
    • B2 Sun: the standard solar models (fluxes, spectra, density and production profiles) and the oscillations inside the Sun.
    • B3 Earth: the crossing of the Earth at night.
    • B4 Interaction: the cross sections, ν–e scattering, CC and NC.
    • B5 Detector: everything folded into interaction rates, then through each detector's published response.
  • Each benchmark fixes its inputs, so tools that compute the same thing can be compared on the same plot.
  • The benchmark pages show what each step varies, how tools agree on it, and how much it moves the rates and the day-night asymmetry.
  • SNUfish is not a product of any collaboration. Please cite the original work of the results you use.

State of the art, legacy and approximations

Not every input SNUfish offers is current. state of the art inputs are the latest calculations and measurements; where several exist (the high- and low-metallicity solar models, the measured Gamow–Teller strengths of ⁴⁰Ar, two radiative-correction calculations) they compete, and their spread is the model range the calculator reports. legacy inputs have been superseded and are kept to reproduce published numbers; approximations simplify a current input. A difference between a legacy or approximate input and the state of the art is not an uncertainty, so the calculator labels both, keeps them out of the model ranges and says when a result uses one. The literature behind each entry: docs/status-of-inputs.md.
